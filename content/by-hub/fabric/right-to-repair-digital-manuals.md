# Right-to-Repair Digital Manuals

## Summary

QR-linked guides embedded in garments to facilitate repair and upcycling.

## Description

Right-to-repair digital manuals embed QR codes, NFC tags, or other digital markers in garments that link to comprehensive repair information including construction diagrams, material specifications, care instructions, and step-by-step repair guides. These digital resources provide consumers and professional repairers with the information needed to maintain, repair, and extend the life of garments, countering the trend toward disposable fashion and planned obsolescence.

This innovation addresses the challenge of garment longevity in an industry that has often prioritized low cost and rapid turnover over durability and repairability. By providing accessible repair information, these systems empower consumers to maintain their clothing and support a culture of repair rather than replacement. The approach aligns with circular economy principles and right-to-repair movements that seek to extend product lifecycles and reduce waste. Some brands are beginning to provide these resources, though adoption remains limited.

The technology is particularly significant as consumers and regulators increasingly demand more sustainable consumption patterns and products designed for longevity. By making repair information easily accessible, digital manuals can help shift fashion culture toward valuing and maintaining clothing rather than discarding it. However, the effectiveness depends on garments being designed for repairability in the first place, requiring changes in both information provision and product design. As right-to-repair regulations emerge and consumer expectations shift, digital repair manuals could become standard features of fashion products.
