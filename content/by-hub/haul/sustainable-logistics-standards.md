# Sustainable Logistics Standards

## Summary

Protocols and technologies for measuring, reporting, and reducing carbon footprints.

## Description

The global logistics industry faces mounting pressure to address its environmental impact, particularly as supply chains account for a significant portion of worldwide carbon emissions. Traditional approaches to measuring and reducing these emissions have been fragmented, with inconsistent methodologies making it difficult to compare performance across companies or accurately track progress toward climate goals. The challenge is especially acute for scope 3 emissions—those generated throughout the entire value chain, from raw material extraction to final delivery—which are notoriously difficult to measure and attribute. Sustainable Logistics Standards emerge as a comprehensive framework to address these challenges, providing standardised protocols and digital tools for measuring, reporting, and ultimately reducing the carbon footprint of global supply chains.

At their core, these standards establish unified methodologies for calculating emissions across different transportation modes, warehousing operations, and packaging processes. They incorporate digital carbon passports—electronic records that track the environmental impact of individual products throughout their journey from manufacturer to end consumer. These passports aggregate data from multiple sources, including fuel consumption records, energy usage in warehouses, and transportation distances, creating a transparent and verifiable account of each product's carbon footprint. The standards also encompass verification systems for alternative fuels, such as sustainable aviation fuel or marine biofuels, ensuring that claims about green fuel usage can be independently validated. By establishing common metrics and reporting frameworks, these standards enable companies to identify emission hotspots within their supply chains and make data-driven decisions about where interventions will have the greatest impact.

Industry adoption of these standards is accelerating as regulatory requirements tighten and corporate sustainability commitments become more ambitious. Early implementations suggest that standardised measurement alone can drive significant improvements, as visibility into emissions data enables logistics providers to optimise routes, consolidate shipments, and transition to lower-emission transportation modes. The standards are increasingly being integrated into procurement decisions, with major retailers and manufacturers requiring suppliers to demonstrate compliance as a condition of doing business. This creates a powerful incentive structure that extends sustainability practices throughout entire supply networks. As the logistics industry works toward net-zero targets, these standards provide the essential infrastructure for credible climate action, transforming sustainability from an aspirational goal into a measurable, manageable aspect of supply chain operations that can be continuously improved over time.
