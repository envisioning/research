# Warmtewet (Heat Market Regulation)

## Summary

Regulation of district heating pricing, ownership, and consumer protection that strongly shapes public acceptance of heat networks in new developments.

## Description

Heat market regulation (e.g., the Dutch Warmtewet and evolving policy) governs who can own and operate district heating networks, how prices are set, and what consumer protections exist. In practice, district heating acceptance often depends less on technology than on trust: monopoly fears and price uncertainty can trigger opposition.

As cities pursue aardgasvrij transitions and dense new builds, heat network governance becomes a decisive factor in both permitting and community acceptance.

The signal represents the institutional layer required to make heat infrastructure socially and politically viable. Key challenges include balancing investor certainty with consumer protection, preventing monopoly abuse, enabling municipal/public ownership models, and aligning heat governance with housing affordability goals.
