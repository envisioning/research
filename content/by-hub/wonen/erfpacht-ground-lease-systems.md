# Erfpacht (Ground Lease) Systems

## Summary

Municipal ground lease models separating land ownership from building ownership, enabling long-term public control over land value and speculation.

## Description

Erfpacht (ground lease) is a system where municipalities retain ownership of land while leasing it to developers or homeowners, typically for 50-99 years. Amsterdam is the most prominent example, with roughly 80% of land under municipal erfpacht. This separates land speculation from housing markets and allows cities to capture land value increases.

The model enables municipalities to set conditions on development, adjust lease terms to policy goals, and prevent speculative land hoarding. Recent reforms in Amsterdam introduced 'eternal' erfpacht with fixed canons, addressing affordability concerns.

The signal represents a structural intervention giving public authorities ongoing control over land use. Key challenges include political resistance from property owners preferring freehold, complexity of canon calculations, and ensuring the system genuinely improves affordability rather than just shifting costs.
