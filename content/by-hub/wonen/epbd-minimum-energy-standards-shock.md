# EPBD / Minimum Energy Standards Shock

## Summary

EU-driven building performance requirements that can trigger mass retrofit obligations—reshaping private rental supply, costs, and political backlash.

## Description

The EU’s building policy direction (e.g., EPBD recast and related minimum performance trajectories) pushes member states toward stricter minimum energy standards and deep renovation. This can rapidly reprice older housing stock and force difficult trade-offs: affordability, heritage constraints, tenant disruption, and capacity shortages.

For Benelux, the risk is a regulation shock: if enforcement is strict but financing and contractor capacity are insufficient, landlords may sell, rents may rise, and political resistance can intensify.

The signal represents how climate policy can unintentionally become a housing supply and legitimacy crisis if not paired with delivery capacity and fairness mechanisms. Key challenges include just transition design, sequencing requirements, and creating financing that reaches low- and middle-income households.
