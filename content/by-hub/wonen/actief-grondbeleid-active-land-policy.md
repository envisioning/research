# Actief Grondbeleid (Active Land Policy)

## Summary

Municipalities buying/servicing land and capturing land value to steer development outcomes (affordability, timing, infrastructure).

## Description

Active land policy (actief grondbeleid) is when public authorities acquire land, service it (infrastructure, remediation), and then dispose of it under conditions (affordable quotas, typologies, phasing). This contrasts with ‘facilitative’ planning where the market assembles land and the municipality mainly regulates.

Active land policy can accelerate delivery by reducing land assembly friction and enabling coordinated infrastructure, while also embedding social value requirements. It also exposes municipalities to financial risk, particularly during downturns.

The signal represents whether Benelux cities have the institutional capacity and political appetite to act as market-shaping developers rather than permit-granting regulators. Key challenges include balance-sheet risk, procurement complexity, and ensuring value capture is reinvested in public goods rather than lost to governance churn.
