# Precision Agriculture for Smallholder Farms

## Summary

AI and satellite-powered precision farming adapted for India's 150 million smallholdings — using smartphone-based soil testing, satellite crop monitoring, and AI advisory to optimize yields on sub-2-hectare plots.

## Description

India is pioneering precision agriculture techniques adapted for smallholder farming contexts that are fundamentally different from Western large-farm precision agriculture. While US precision ag assumes GPS-guided tractors on thousand-acre farms, Indian precision ag works with smartphone apps, satellite imagery, and drone data on 1-2 hectare plots farmed by hand or with small tools. Companies like Fasal, SatSure, and CropIn have developed affordable sensor-and-AI systems for this context.

The technology stack includes: satellite-based crop health monitoring (using freely available Sentinel and Landsat imagery), smartphone-based soil testing kits, AI-powered crop disease identification from photographs, weather-based irrigation advisory, and market price prediction. These tools are delivered via simple mobile interfaces, often in local languages, to farmers who may have limited formal education but are increasingly smartphone-literate.

The impact potential is enormous. If precision agriculture techniques can improve yields by even 10-15% for India's smallholder farmers — who grow 80% of India's food — the effect on food security, farmer incomes, and agricultural sustainability would be transformative. India is essentially developing a new paradigm: precision agriculture for the Global South, where farms are small, capital is scarce, and labor is abundant. Solutions proven in India could scale to smallholder farming contexts across Africa, Southeast Asia, and Latin America.
