# Precision Agriculture & Satellite-Guided Farming

## Summary

Mexican agritech startups deploy drone imaging, soil sensors, and satellite analytics to optimize irrigation in water-scarce northern farming regions and avocado production zones.

## Description

Mexico's agricultural sector — the world's leading avocado producer and a major exporter of berries, tomatoes, and peppers — is adopting precision farming technologies to address water scarcity and optimize yields. Drone-based multispectral imaging identifies crop stress before it's visible to the naked eye, soil moisture sensors enable deficit irrigation that saves 30-40% water, and satellite-derived NDVI maps guide variable-rate fertilizer application across large farm operations.

The technology stack is adapted to Mexico's specific challenges: highly variable rainfall patterns, limited groundwater reserves in northern states, and the need to maintain export-quality produce standards. Startups combine local agronomic knowledge with off-the-shelf sensor hardware and proprietary analytics platforms — essentially applying Silicon Valley precision agriculture concepts to tropical and semi-arid farming conditions.

The strategic dimension is water security. Northern Mexico's farming regions face severe drought stress, and climate change projections indicate worsening conditions. Precision agriculture isn't just about yield optimization — it's about maintaining agricultural viability in regions that may otherwise become unproductive. Mexico's $40B+ agricultural export industry depends on technological adaptation to water constraints.
