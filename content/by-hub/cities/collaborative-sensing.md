# Collaborative Sensing

## Summary

Networked sensors using AI to coordinate data and decisions across urban mobility, environment, and infrastructure.

## Description

Collaborative sensing refers to networks of sensors that share data and are coordinated—often with AI or optimisation algorithms—to achieve system-level outcomes rather than isolated readings. Individual nodes may be cameras, environmental sensors, traffic detectors, or industrial instruments; connectivity and central or edge processing allow fusion, inference, and control. In urban mobility, for example, traffic signals can adapt in real time using feeds from cameras and environmental sensors to reduce congestion and local pollution. In mining or construction, networked sensors support mapping, safety monitoring, and resource tracking. In weather and climate, distributed sensing improves nowcasting and storm analysis.

The technology addresses the limit of siloed sensors: single-point data may be insufficient for decisions that depend on context, correlation, or prediction. By pooling data and applying models, collaborative sensing can improve situational awareness, automate responses, and optimise resource use. Use cases extend to smart buildings, agriculture, and critical infrastructure. Advances in IoT connectivity, edge compute, and machine learning have made it feasible to deploy and coordinate larger sensor networks at acceptable cost.

Challenges include interoperability, latency, privacy (especially when data is personal or sensitive), and governance of shared infrastructure. As cities and organisations invest in sensor networks and digital twins, collaborative sensing will increasingly underpin operational and planning decisions, shifting the value proposition from data collection alone to integrated sensing-and-action systems.
