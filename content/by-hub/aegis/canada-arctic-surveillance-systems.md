# Integrated Arctic Surveillance Systems

## Summary

Canada is building multi-layered Arctic surveillance combining RADARSAT satellites, underwater sensors, and autonomous systems to monitor the Northwest Passage as climate change opens Arctic shipping routes.

## Description

As climate change opens the Northwest Passage to increasing commercial shipping and military activity, Canada is developing integrated surveillance systems combining satellite radar (RADARSAT Constellation), underwater acoustic sensors, autonomous underwater vehicles, ground-based radar, and patrol aircraft into a comprehensive Arctic domain awareness network. The system aims to monitor maritime traffic, environmental changes, and potential security threats across Canada's vast Northern territory.

Arctic surveillance matters because Canada claims sovereignty over the Northwest Passage as internal waters — a claim contested by the US and other nations who consider it an international strait. As the passage becomes navigable for longer periods each year, the ability to demonstrate effective monitoring and control is essential to maintaining this sovereignty claim. Environmental monitoring is equally critical as Arctic ecosystems undergo rapid change.

The strategic stakes are immense. The Arctic contains an estimated 13% of the world's undiscovered oil, 30% of undiscovered natural gas, and vast mineral resources. Control over Arctic shipping routes could reshape global trade patterns. Canada's ability to monitor and assert sovereignty over its Northern territory will determine whether it captures the economic benefits of Arctic opening or becomes a bystander in its own backyard.
