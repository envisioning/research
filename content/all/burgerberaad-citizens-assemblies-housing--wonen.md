# Burgerberaad (Citizens' Assemblies) for Housing

## Summary

Randomly selected citizen panels debating housing dilemmas, bypassing polarized NIMBY/YIMBY dynamics to find consensus.

## Description

Burgerberaad (Citizens' Assemblies) are increasingly used in Benelux cities to tackle complex dilemmas like housing density vs. greenery. Unlike town halls dominated by vocal opponents, these assemblies use random selection (sortition) to create a representative microcosm of the population to deliberate on policy.

They have been used to generate mandates for difficult decisions, such as increasing density or repurposing public space. This deliberative democracy tool bypasses the polarized 'referendum' dynamic.

The signal represents a sophisticated evolution of community engagement from consultation to deliberation. Key challenges include political commitment to actually implement the assembly's recommendations.
