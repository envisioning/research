# Skills Wallets & Learning Records

## Summary

Portable, verifiable records of skills across school, work, and life.

## Description

The fragmentation of learning credentials across multiple institutions, employers, and platforms has created a persistent challenge in modern education and workforce development. Traditional transcripts and resumes fail to capture the full spectrum of an individual's capabilities, often overlooking skills gained through workplace training, online courses, volunteer work, or self-directed learning. This gap becomes particularly problematic as the pace of technological change accelerates and employers increasingly seek specific competencies rather than generic degree credentials. Skills wallets and comprehensive learning records emerge as a solution to this fragmentation, offering a unified, portable system for documenting and verifying all forms of learning throughout a person's lifetime.

At their core, these systems function as digital repositories that aggregate credentials, certifications, badges, and competency assessments from diverse sources into a single, user-controlled record. Unlike traditional transcripts that merely list courses completed, skills wallets capture granular information about specific competencies, the contexts in which they were developed, and evidence of their application. The technical foundation typically relies on blockchain or distributed ledger technologies to ensure tamper-proof verification, while standardised data formats enable machine-readability across different platforms and systems. Individuals maintain ownership and control over their records, deciding what information to share and with whom, while credential issuers—whether universities, employers, or training providers—can cryptographically sign their attestations to prevent fraud. This architecture creates a trusted ecosystem where skills can be verified instantly without requiring manual confirmation from issuing institutions.

Early implementations of skills wallets are emerging across education systems and workforce development programs, particularly in regions facing acute skills mismatches or rapid economic transitions. Some national governments are piloting comprehensive learning record systems that integrate formal education credentials with vocational training and micro-credentials earned through online platforms. In the corporate sector, forward-thinking employers are beginning to recognise skills wallets as tools for internal talent mobility, enabling workers to showcase capabilities that might not be reflected in their job titles or formal qualifications. The technology also supports more sophisticated matching algorithms that can connect individuals with learning opportunities, job openings, or project assignments based on their demonstrated competencies rather than proxy measures like degrees or years of experience. As the future of work increasingly demands continuous learning and career flexibility, skills wallets represent a critical infrastructure for enabling individuals to navigate complex learning and employment landscapes while providing employers and educators with more accurate signals of human capability.
