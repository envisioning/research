# District Cooling Systems

## Summary

Centralized cooling infrastructure serving entire districts, critical for GCC's extreme climate and mega-developments.

## Description

District cooling systems provide centralized air conditioning to multiple buildings through a network of chilled water pipes, dramatically reducing energy consumption compared to individual building systems. In the GCC context, where cooling accounts for up to 70% of electricity demand, these systems are essential infrastructure for sustainable urban development.

Major implementations include Dubai's Palm Jumeirah, Lusail City in Qatar, and Saudi Arabia's NEOM project. The technology enables developers to reduce building footprints (no rooftop chillers), cut energy costs by 40-50%, and achieve sustainability targets. UAE leads GCC adoption with Empower and Tabreed operating some of the world's largest district cooling networks.

The signal represents the shift from building-level to district-level infrastructure thinking, enabling higher density development while meeting net-zero commitments. Key challenges include high upfront capital costs and the need for integrated urban planning from project inception.
